## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary: the curious general public.** People with no academic background in decolonial thought who encounter Decolonial Dialogues through Instagram or word of mouth, follow a link, and have to decide whether this is for someone like them. Their job on the site is to work out what the four days actually are, whether they'd be out of place, and what it costs — then commit to a trip abroad worth €399–499 plus flights and accommodation.

The programme's own copy names the tension directly: *"This isn't just for academics. Everyone's invited no matter your background or experience."* Academics and practitioners do attend and are welcome, but they are not the visitor the site is designed around. If a first-time visitor with no field vocabulary can't tell whether they belong, the site has failed regardless of who else it satisfies.

## Product Purpose

Decolonial Dialogues is a four-day residential course and dialogue series held in Granada, Andalucía, Spain, running twice a year — one April edition and one October edition. It teaches the history of modern colonialism from the conquest of Al-Ándalus through to Palestine today, alongside the decolonization of knowledge and spirituality, and combines lectures with field trips into Granada and the Albaicín.

decolonialdialogues.org is the **permanent public home of the series** — not a campaign page for a single edition. It outlives each cohort: it presents whichever edition is next, and carries the faculty, the participant reflections, and the record of past editions across years.

Success is a visitor who arrived curious and unsure, understood what the programme is and that they belong in it, and wrote to `cit@dialogoglobal.com` to book a place.

## Positioning

- **The history is taught where it happened.** Granada is not a venue choice; it is the primary text. The Albaicín and the city are field trips, not excursions. A neighbouring programme could copy the reading list but not the ground.
- **One continuous arc, Al-Ándalus to Palestine.** The through-line — that 1492 and the present-day conflict in Palestine belong to a single history of modern colonialism — is the intellectual claim the programme is organized around.
- **International faculty, no institutional gatekeeping.** Scholars of standing (Ramón Grosfoguel, UC Berkeley; Katya Colmenares, Dussel Library Series) teaching in a setting with no admissions bar, no credential, and no prerequisite. The programme's stated principle: *"Knowledge doesn't belong to institutions. It belongs to the people."*
- **Scholarship, spirituality and music in one frame.** Islamic spiritual ecology and a private Al Firdaus concert of Andalusian Morisco music sit inside the curriculum rather than beside it.

## Operating Context

- **Cadence:** two editions per year — April and October.
- **Next edition:** October 29 – November 1, 2026. Granada, Andalucía, Spain.
- **Price:** €499 full. €399 early rate (20% off), available July 1 – September 15. *As of this record's writing (August 2026) the early rate is live and expires September 15, 2026.*
- **Included:** daily lectures & talks, walks, guided tour visits in Granada City, private concert.
- **Not included:** flights, airport transfer, accommodation, most meals. Participants arrange their own travel and lodging — a real planning burden the site should not gloss over.
- **Booking:** by email to `cit@dialogoglobal.com`, handled by a person. There is no checkout, no payment processor, and no on-site registration form.
- **Distribution:** primarily Instagram; the site is the destination a social link lands on.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **No backend, no payments, no CMS.** The single conversion action is an email to `cit@dialogoglobal.com`. Nothing on the site should imply instant booking or online payment.
- **Content must be structured for recurrence.** Editions repeat every April and October. Dates, price, faculty, and curriculum are data that change per edition, not prose hardcoded to October 2026.
- **Stack is already committed** by the existing scaffold; no framework decision is open.
- **Undecided — do not resolve by invention:**
  - Accommodation and travel guidance for participants (excluded from the price, absent from the source).
  - The day-by-day schedule, session times, and venue names.
  - Cohort size / capacity.
  - Language of instruction on site (the website is English-only by decision; the delivery language of the programme is not stated in the source).

## Product Principles

1. **The open door comes before the credentials.** The programme's core promise is that no background is required. Anything that reads as academic gatekeeping — dense jargon, an insider tone, faculty credentials leading the page — contradicts the product it is selling.
2. **Granada is the argument, not the backdrop.** Situated learning is the mechanism. Place, and the specific ground of Al-Ándalus, carry the teaching.
3. **One booking path, and it is unmissable.** The only conversion is an email to a human. It must be reachable from anywhere without hunting, and must never look like a checkout.
4. **Built for the next edition, and the one after.** Editions recur every April and October. Nothing should have to be rewritten to swap October 2026 for April 2027.
5. **Never claim more than the source supports.** Every fact traces to the prospectus or to something the organizers confirmed. Absences stay absent.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The audience is explicitly people with no prior exposure to the field, which makes plain language a product requirement rather than a style preference: terms like *coloniality*, *modernity/coloniality*, and *decolonization of knowledge* must be usable by someone meeting them for the first time. Visitors are also planning international travel and will read on phones in transit.

No formal conformance standard was established by the organizers.
